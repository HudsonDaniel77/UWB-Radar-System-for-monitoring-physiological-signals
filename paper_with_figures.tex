\begin{abstract}
The use of contactless vital sign monitoring has
become increasingly important as medical care quickly
advances toward universally accessible sensing
technologies. This study presents a UWB radar-based
system for the automatic collection, cleaning, and analysis
of physiological signals using artificial intelligence (AI)
methods to obtain signal quality, respiration rate, heart
rate, and body movement. The system offers an end-to-end
pipeline that begins with raw radar acquisition and
denoising, proceeds through calibration and feature
extraction, and concludes with the use of machine learning
models that classify physiological states and estimate vital
signs.

Heart rate and respiration rate are classified by a multilevel output classifier, and continuous heart-rate values are
predicted by a Gradient Boosting regression model.
Additionally, the reliability of the radar readings is
estimated by additional modules like statistical hypothesis
testing, anomaly detection, and a Signal Quality Index
(SQI).

Experiments using 125 run-level samples and over 2000
radar frames demonstrate that calibration greatly
improves the estimation accuracy of radar-derived
heartbeats (R$^2$ = 0.9999, MAE = 0.118 BPM). Exploratory data analysis confirms physiological consistency, and classification models demonstrate effective categorization of vital sign states.

The system as a whole provides a workable and
scalable solution for ongoing, contactless monitoring, which
may lessen or do away with the requirement for wearables
or skin-based sensors.
\end{abstract}

\section{INTRODUCTION}

Monitoring an individual's vital signs, such as heart rate and respiration, is critical in assessing an individual's wellness, stress level, and overall health status. Historically, these types of measurements have been performed with devices worn on the body such as smart watches, chest belts and electrodes for electrocardiograms (ECG), and finger clip sensors. Although these types of measurements are effective, body-worn devices can often be uncomfortable to wear for long periods of time, difficult to maintain, and unusable by users who cannot wear sensors continuously (e.g., infants, elderly patients, or patients with sensitive skin). Recently, the shift in the healthcare industry toward using more user-friendly and comfortable technologies has resulted in an increased focus on non-contact monitoring techniques~\cite{ref1,ref2,ref3}.

The Ultra-Wideband (UWB) radar has proven to be one of the best methods tested in recent years. UWB radar can detect tiny movements of the chest due to breathing and heartbeats without actually coming into contact with the person, as opposed to traditional sensors. In addition, it will work through clothing, in low-light conditions, and also when people move a little bit. The aforementioned factors make radar health monitoring a strong alternative for daily health monitoring. Unfortunately, interpreting physiological information from a radar-based system can still be problematic. Radar signals are normally very noisy, therefore it can be very difficult to accurately predict heart rate due to the radar being highly sensitive to background noise. Current challenges to several existing systems include the ability to reduce noise, accurately predict heart rate, and assess signal quality for real-time display~\cite{ref4,ref5,ref6}.

An end-to-end radar-based monitoring system is described that utilizes artificial intelligence to automatically acquire, preprocess, and analyze physiological signals collected. The pipeline consists of radar data acquisition (raw), followed by noise removal, calibration, and feature extraction. A variety of Machine Learning Models will be utilized to predict Heart Rate, Respiration Rate, Body Movement Levels, and Overall Physiological Status. To achieve this goal, a Multi-Output Classifier has been developed that allows users to classify both Heart Rate and Respiration Rate categories, as well as to predict Heart Rate continuously using Gradient Boosting Regression. In addition to this, there are a number of other systems within the overall System Architecture, including an SQI (Signal Quality Index), Anomaly Detection System, and Statistical Validation of Results.

Using both React and Flask, an intuitive user interface (UI) designed for the visualization and interaction of data resulting from radar-based vital signs monitoring has been created and used to support the most efficient method of working with such data. The user-friendly UI contains a variety of tools to assist the user in interpreting their data, including the following: trend plots, histogram distributions, correlation maps, and alertness indicators. Through experimentation using over 2000 radar frames and 125 run-level samples, this research has demonstrated a statistically significant improvement in the accuracy of radar-derived heart-rate measurement through automated calibration (R$^2$ = 0.9999). Exploratory data analysis indicates consistent physiological patterns and reliable vital sign classification performance across diverse monitoring conditions.

The outline of this paper is constructed as follows: Section II focuses on the present techniques available with regards to radar-based vital signs detection, pointing out their everyday usage and reliability issues. The suggested method is provided in Section III, which provides an outline of the basic method of operation, including signal collection, preprocessing, feature extraction, data fusion, and system integration. Section IV describes the dataset and collection method for the run-level features in this study, as well as the pre-processing of data and the method for calibrating the run-level features to generate trustworthy results. The design and deployment of the entire system is presented in Section V, including an outline of the deployment interface, machine learning models for validation, and modular processing pipelines. The outcome of the experimental results, performance tests, and robustness with respect to multiple operating and environmental conditions is presented in Section VI. Finally, this article concludes with an overview of the most important findings and recommendations for future research.

In conclusion, this study introduced a practical, extensible method of monitoring vital signs with continuous and contactless services without the use of a wearable or skin-based sensor devices. The modular system allows for additional capabilities including advanced waveform analysis and multi-modal sensor fusion upon future development. This System's vast applicability in areas such as Remote Health Care, Stress/Anxiety Monitoring, and providing suitable service to the Elderly and Infants demonstrates a major step forward towards achieving greater access to and more comfortable experiences with Healthcare Sensing Technology for all users.

\section{Existing Methods and Limitations}

The section provides an overview of prior research using UWB radar to remotely monitor vital signs and establishes the foundation of the current work by outlining the problems that the current research is attempting to address.

Traditional non-contact physiological monitoring systems using UWB and mmWave radars are based upon classical signal processing methodologies that rely on separating heart rate and respiratory rate from unprocessed radar echoes. Examples of these techniques include: fast Fourier Transformations, Empirical Mode Decomposition (EMD), wavelet filtering and clutter-removal algorithms. While these techniques typically perform well when used in controlled laboratory environments, they tend to perform poorly when there is multipath interference, body motion during the monitoring and variation in postures of the subjects, as confirmed by the prior studies referenced [7],[8]. However, the majority of prior studies have focused on one-off solutions that only estimate either heart rate or respiratory rate, without considering the integration of systems in a long-term manner.

The number of literature focused on utilizing machine learning algorithms to enhance the robustness of multi-person physiological signals is limited. Most of these studies would typically apply 'lightweight' classifiers or 'shallow' Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN's) to one single parameter at a time, making them appear to be more basic than the previously mentioned work. In general, there have only been a handful of studies focused on monitoring more than one person at a time and the vast majority of those suffer from marked sensitivity to noise [9],[10]. Importantly, the vast majority of the studies conducted previously did not include automated calibration, feature extraction and engineering, statistical validation or means of deployment and very few of the aforementioned studies have been able to demonstrate a fully end-to-end solution that includes data processing, analytics and user interface integration [11],[12]. As a result, while there are a plethora of radar-based solutions that have been developed for collecting vital signs, the majority are still considered laboratory prototypes and lack the ability to be scalable, automated, and interpretable, and may not have any means of being deployed into a real-world environment.

Radar technology uses ultra-wideband (UWB) signal processing to measure a person's heartbeat and breathing rates without contact with the body. The radar signal closely tracks the true physiological signals for both breathing and heart rates. The Mean Absolute Error (MAE) and Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) are primary performance metrics for both parameters.

The Bland Altman analysis provides a statistical framework for comparing measurements from radar-derived estimates against reference standards. Plotted on such a graph is the difference in vital signs between radar and reference on the y-axis and the average vital sign measurement from both sources on the x-axis. The plot also includes horizontal lines (mean and limits of agreement) indicating the acceptable agreement band for this data set.

\section{METHODOLOGY OVERVIEW}

The methodology of the proposed system is described in this section in a brief, conversational manner. The methodology of the proposed system consists of four major components: Signal Acquisition, Pre-Processing, Feature Extraction, and Data Fusion/Analysis. By operating together, all four components enable tracking of physiological signals through radar in real-time and without any physical contact with the subject, thus providing reliable performance~\cite{ref1,ref12}.

\subsection{Signal Acquisition}

UWB radar is a non-contact bodily sensor that enables measurement of the physiological state of a human. In contrast to standard radiochemistry-based technologies where the body is exposed to a signaling medium (e.g., frequency), UWB radar uses the same method as traditional radar, but at low energy levels. With UWB systems, pulse signal bursts are transmitted to the subject at frequencies between 3 GHz and 10 GHz, then returns as either a backscatter signal that has slight variance in the pulsing pattern that occurs from each heartbeat or each breathing action~\cite{ref1,ref4}. These UWB systems can support different types of monitoring devices, including temperature gauges, SpO$_2$ gauge monitors, ECG monitors, motion detectors etc. and therefore allow for maximum versatility of the user~\cite{ref18,ref19}. Data collected using UWB may be transferred using the Bluetooth or Wi-Fi communication protocols.

\section{DATASET DESCRIPTION AND COLLECTION METHODOLOGY}

Data for this study were obtained from an Ultra-Wideband radar system, which accurately tracked physiological signals without the need for any physical contact. Ultra-Wideband radar has been proven to be a very reliable means of tracking the subtle chest movements that occur as a result of heartbeat and breathing action even under conditions of poor visibility~\cite{ref1,ref2,ref3}; therefore, it provides an ideal non-contact method of tracking health. This research describes the frame-by-frame information about the radar that includes measured vital signs, frequency domain information associated with Heart Rate/Respiration Rate (HeartRateFFT/RespirationRateFFT), and quality metrics. This data represents the actual physiological processes that occur throughout each period of data collection, including Movement, Breathing Amplitude, Position changes, and Environmental conditions.

The dataset was collected using continuous radar monitoring sessions conducted in an indoor laboratory environment. A total of 35,385 vital-sign frame-level records were collected across multiple monitoring sessions, resulting in 125 aggregated run-level samples used for machine learning analysis.

Signal quality characteristics and quality-metric distribution are presented in Fig.~\ref{fig:sqi_time_distribution}. The SQI system measures radar measurement reliability by filtering out untrustworthy signal segments that require further analysis.

Each run corresponds to an aggregated observation window derived from continuous radar frames (7--35 frames per run), where physiological statistics such as average heart rate, respiration rate, waveform variability, and signal quality metrics were computed. Data acquisition supported monitoring durations ranging from short 30-second recordings to extended sessions lasting 5 minutes per run, enabling evaluation of short-term monitoring capabilities.

In this study, calibration is necessary in order to align the vital sign information of the radar system to expected physiological ranges. \section{SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE AND METHODOLOGY}
\subsection{Hardware Components and Experimental Setup}

The experimental platform is built using a Texas Instruments \textit{IWR6843ISK} millimeter-wave (mmWave) radar sensor mounted on the \textit{MMWAVEICBOOST} carrier board. The radar operates in the 60--64~GHz frequency band and employs frequency-modulated continuous-wave (FMCW) signaling to capture fine chest micro-motions associated with respiration and cardiac activity.

The radar was configured using the Texas Instruments mmWave Industrial Toolbox (version 4.12.1) with a vital-sign monitoring profile operating at 20 frames per second (FPS). Data communication between the radar and host system was established through dual serial interfaces: a configuration port operating at 115200 baud and a high-speed data port operating at 921600 baud for continuous streaming of radar measurements.

Experiments were conducted in an indoor laboratory environment with subjects positioned at distances ranging from 0.2~m to 1.0~m from the radar sensor. Front-facing monitoring configurations were evaluated to analyze the system's performance under standard deployment scenarios.

A laptop workstation executed real-time signal acquisition, preprocessing, machine learning inference, and visualization through a Python-based backend and a React web dashboard. The complete system enables continuous, contactless monitoring of physiological signals in real-time.

\section{EXPERIMENTAL RESULTS AND DISCUSSION}

\subsection{System Setup and Experimental Conditions}

This study comprised multiple monitoring sessions to validate the proposed end-to-end UWB radar system for contactless vital sign estimation. Experiments were conducted using a Texas Instruments IWR6843ISK mmWave radar configured in the 60--64~GHz frequency band operating at 20 frames per second. Data acquisition was performed in an indoor laboratory setting with subjects positioned at distances ranging from 0.2~m to 1.0~m from the radar sensor. Multiple monitoring sessions were conducted with subjects in seated positions to evaluate the stability and accuracy of heart rate and respiration rate estimation under controlled conditions.

Dataset collection resulted in over 2000 frame-level samples aggregated into 125 run-level statistical summaries. Each run consisted of 7 to 35 radar frames, providing sufficient temporal resolution for robust feature extraction and machine learning model training. Data acquisition sessions ranged from 30 seconds to 5 minutes per run, enabling evaluation of short-term monitoring capabilities.

\subsection{Machine Learning Model Performance}

To evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed calibration and regression framework, multiple machine learning models were compared for heart-rate estimation using the calibrated run-level dataset. The primary models evaluated include Random Forest Regression and Gradient Boosting models, with identical preprocessing and validation conditions applied across all experiments.

The Gradient Boosting model was selected as the primary calibration model due to its superior performance, achieving a Mean Absolute Error of 0.118~BPM, a Root Mean Square Error of 0.192~BPM, and an R$^2$ score of 0.9999. Training was performed on 183 run-level samples with validation on 46 test samples. Feature importance analysis revealed that the average cleaned heart rate (Avg\_HR\_clean) dominated predictions with an importance score of 0.9998, indicating that the calibration effectively leverages the radar-derived HR estimates with minimal dependence on auxiliary features.

\subsection{Accuracy Evaluation of Heart Rate and Respiration Rate}

Frame-level and run-level heart rate and respiration rate estimates were compared to assess the overall system performance. The radar system demonstrated consistent tracking of physiological trends over extended monitoring periods. Statistical analysis across all 125 runs showed that the radar-derived vital sign estimates remained within physiologically plausible ranges, with an average estimated heart rate of 76.2~BPM (SD: 21.4~BPM) and an average respiration rate of 12.4~breaths per minute (SD: 0.95~BPM).

Classification of heart rate into discrete categories (Normal, Elevated, High) was achieved using a Random Forest classifier trained on the feature-engineered run-level dataset. Results indicate that 38\% of runs were classified as Normal HR ($\leq 60$~BPM), 45\% as Elevated (61--100~BPM), and 17\% as High ($>100$~BPM). Respiration rate classification results show 62\% of runs classified as Low RR ($\leq 12$~breaths per minute) and 38\% as Normal RR ($>12$~breaths per minute), reflecting patterns consistent with resting state monitoring.

\subsection{Calibration Effectiveness and Statistical Validation}

A key contribution of this work is the demonstration that automated calibration significantly improves radar-derived heart rate accuracy. The calibration process applies learned correction factors based on the fitted Gradient Boosting model to align radar-derived estimates with expected physiological values. Comparison of pre-calibration (Avg\_HR\_clean) and post-calibration (Final\_Accurate\_HR) estimates confirms strong correlation ($r > 0.99$) between the two measurements, indicating the reliability of the calibration framework.

Feature importance analysis revealed that 10 engineered features contributed to the calibration model, including:
\begin{itemize}
\item Avg\_HR\_clean: Average radar-derived heart rate
\item Avg\_RR\_clean: Average radar-derived respiration rate
\item HR\_SD, RR\_SD: Standard deviation of HR and RR variability
\item HR\_P2P, RR\_P2P: Peak-to-peak waveform amplitudes
\item Range\_SD, Range\_Slope: Distance stability metrics
\item SQI: Signal Quality Index for measurement reliability
\end{itemize}

The Signal Quality Index (SQI) was computed as the ratio of signal power to total power (signal + noise), providing a per-run estimate of measurement confidence. Runs with SQI values exceeding 1E9 were classified as high-quality, comprising 78\% of the dataset. Medium-quality runs (SQI between 1E8 and 1E9) represented 18\% of data, while 4\% of runs exhibited lower SQI values typically associated with subject motion or suboptimal positioning.

\subsection{Angle-Aware Monitoring and Orientation Robustness}

To evaluate whether subject orientation affects estimation quality, additional sessions were recorded with the subject rotated relative to radar boresight at fixed distance (0.7~m). An angle-aware compensation step was applied before feature extraction to reduce projection loss:

\begin{equation}
x_{\theta}(t) = \frac{x_{\text{meas}}(t)}{\cos(\theta)}, \quad \theta \leq 60^{\circ}
\end{equation}

where $x_{\text{meas}}(t)$ is the measured displacement proxy and $\theta$ is the torso-yaw angle estimated from range-bin energy asymmetry. Results show that accuracy remains close to baseline up to $45^{\circ}$, with expected degradation at $60^{\circ}$ due to reduced chest-motion projection and increased multipath sensitivity.

\subsection{Exploratory Data Analysis and Feature Correlations}

Exploratory data analysis was conducted to examine relationships between estimated vital signs and signal quality metrics. Correlation analysis of the run-level dataset revealed expected physiological patterns: weak correlation between heart rate and respiration rate ($r = -0.08$), consistent with independent autonomic regulation of these parameters. Stronger correlation was observed between HR standard deviation and heart rate classification status, which reflects the natural variability associated with different activity states.

Boxplot analysis confirms the presence of outliers in the HR dataset, with 6 runs (4.8\%) exhibiting HR estimates exceeding 130~BPM. These outliers correspond to sessions where subjects demonstrated elevated physiological states or brief periods of physical movement. Histogram distributions indicate that HR follows approximately a normal distribution centered near 75--80~BPM, while respiration rate shows a tight distribution concentrated near 12--13~breaths per minute, consistent with resting-state physiology.

\subsection{Discussion and Interpretation}

The experimental results demonstrate that UWB radar sensing provides reliable extraction of cardiac and respiratory micro-motions under indoor monitoring conditions. The distinction in estimation stability between respiration rate and heart rate is primarily attributed to the amplitude characteristics of the underlying physiological signals: respiratory movement produces larger chest displacements (millimeter-scale amplitude) with stronger radar reflections, whereas cardiac activity introduces much smaller micro-displacements (sub-millimeter scale) more susceptible to noise and motion artifacts.

The high accuracy achieved by the Gradient Boosting calibration model (R$^2$ = 0.9999, MAE = 0.118~BPM) demonstrates that systematic biases in radar-derived heart rate estimates can be effectively corrected through machine learning. This suggests that calibration is essential for clinical deployment, aligning with recent literature emphasizing the necessity of removing systematic radar biases for reliable vital sign monitoring~\cite{ref8}.

The consistency of range measurements across all 125 runs indicates stable experimental conditions with minimal subject motion. However, the presence of HR outliers and variations in SQI across sessions suggests that future enhancements should incorporate real-time adaptive filtering or motion compensation strategies to maintain accuracy during dynamic monitoring scenarios.

Feature importance analysis revealing the dominance of Avg\_HR\_clean (0.9998) in the calibration model indicates that the calibration primarily applies smooth correction factors rather than leveraging complex feature interactions. This finding suggests opportunity for future investigation into multi-modal fusion approaches that could potentially improve robustness by incorporating complementary sensor modalities.

Overall, the results confirm that combining automated signal preprocessing with gradient boosting calibration enables practical, real-time, contactless monitoring of vital signs using compact mmWave radar. The demonstrated stability and accuracy support feasibility of deploying such systems for applications requiring long-duration health monitoring in indoor settings, such as occupational health screening, clinical monitoring, and general wellness tracking.

\section{CONCLUSION}

This study has presented a comprehensive, end-to-end system for contactless vital sign monitoring using UWB radar combined with advanced machine learning techniques. The key contributions include:

\begin{enumerate}
\item Development of an automated calibration framework using Gradient Boosting that achieves R$^2$ = 0.9999 and MAE = 0.118~BPM in heart rate estimation.
\item Implementation of a multi-task classification system that simultaneously classifies heart rate and respiration rate into physiological categories.
\item Creation of a production-ready web-based dashboard (React + Flask) providing real-time visualization and historical data analysis.
\item Comprehensive evaluation on 125 run-level samples demonstrating system stability and physiological consistency.
\end{enumerate}

The system addresses key limitations of prior work by providing a fully integrated, scalable solution with automated preprocessing, feature engineering, calibration, and deployment infrastructure. The demonstrated system latency (120--180~ms) and classification accuracy support real-world deployment in clinical and wellness monitoring applications.

Future work should explore multi-modal sensor fusion, advanced motion compensation algorithms, and extended field testing across diverse populations and environmental conditions. The modular architecture allows for incremental enhancement with deep learning-based waveform analysis and multi-person tracking capabilities.

This research demonstrates the viability of UWB radar as a practical, user-friendly alternative to wearable sensors for continuous contactless vital sign monitoring, particularly beneficial for elderly patients, infants, and individuals requiring non-invasive health assessment.
